BL157.6 poll A/B: the rule of the decision, written and committed BEFORE any session of the run (Wed Nov 11 2026). `scripts/playback-poll-ab.py run` refuses to
start until this file is committed and unchanged, and writes its hash and commit into the run's `manifest.json`.

What is decided: whether `TESSEL_PLAYBACK_POLL=deadline` (the playback clock is asked when the next frame is due, and at least twice per frame) may replace `fixed`
(every half frame after the previous answer) as the editor's default. It is about the evenness of the delivered pictures and how soon a changed frame is requested.
It is NOT about the 60-160 ms pauses BL157 is about, and it is NOT an acceptance by the user.

How (one command; the SAME binary for both arms):
  python3 scripts/playback-poll-ab.py run --binary target/release/tessel-editor --project <project document> --out <scratch dir> --pairs 12 --report-dir <this folder>
  24 sessions, each a fresh editor that plays the whole project twice (a first play of a cold editor and a repeat), the arm order fixed/deadline, deadline/fixed, ...
  The arms differ ONLY in the environment variable TESSEL_PLAYBACK_POLL (`fixed` | `deadline`); asset I/O `sync` (the shipping default), host trace on,
  both first-use factors explicitly 0. Pictures are the gaps between consecutive pictures the monitor finished inside a play; the change lag is how long after
  its boundary a frame change was requested, from the clock the host traced (`tick.clock`).

What the model predicts at 24 fps (`crates/desktop/src/workstation/playback_poll_tests.rs`, which reproduces the native measurements for `fixed` first):
  fixed     13% of the pictures under 30 ms after the previous one, mean change lag 11.1 ms, 1.87 polls per frame change
  deadline  0% under 30 ms, mean change lag 2.5 ms, 2.00 polls per frame change
The rule below does not assume the model is right: it asks the real editor.

DECISION RULES, fixed here (and in `docs/viewport-real-latency-ab-results/poll-ab/rule.txt`) before any measurement:

    counted        a session that is valid (the harness's `session_validity`), did not fail and has its two plays. A pair is counted when both its sessions are.
    INVALID ARMS   a play whose `play_clock_started.poll` is not its arm, or a session that played without recording one: no verdict (a session
                   that died before its first play is `failed`, not this).
    INCONCLUSIVE   fewer than 10 counted pairs (of the 12 asked for): no verdict.
    CONTROL NOT REPRODUCED
                   fewer than 8% of the `fixed` arm's pictures came less than 30 ms after the previous one (the 72 native sessions: 11-23%): the pattern
                   under repair is not in this run, which then says nothing about the repair.
    REGRESSION     (control reproduced) in the `deadline` arm, compared with the `fixed` arm, ANY of:
                     - the share of pictures more than 62.5 ms after the previous one is more than 0.25 percentage points higher;
                     - the count of pictures more than 100 ms after the previous one is above 1.5 times the fixed arm's (scaled to the number of plays
                       of the deadline arm) plus 3;
                     - skipped frames per play are more than 0.15 higher;
                     - it has more hung sessions (a play that shows under 90% of the frames, or waits over 2000 ms between pictures) than the fixed arm.
    NO GAIN        (no regression) ANY of, for the `deadline` arm:
                     - more than 3% of the pictures came less than 30 ms after the previous one;
                     - the mean change lag (how long after its boundary a frame change was requested, from the clock the host traced) is above 4 ms,
                       or less than 5 ms below the fixed arm's;
                     - polls per frame change outside 1.9-2.15;
                     - the mean change lag is not lower in at least 90% of the counted pairs.
    ADOPT          none of the above: the evenness gain is real and nothing measured got worse. It permits making `deadline` the default; it is not
                   an acceptance of BL157 and the long pauses are untouched.

The numbers are diagnostic marks chosen from the noise of the 72 earlier native sessions (the share of pictures over 62.5 ms was 0.86-0.90%, with a standard
error of about 0.11 percentage points for a difference of 12 sessions per arm), not limits approved by anyone. The outcome is mechanical; nothing here is changed
by what the numbers turn out to be, and an outcome other than ADOPT leaves the default as it is.
